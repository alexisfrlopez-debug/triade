\paragraph{Structured outputs and constrained decoding.}
The constrained-generation side of the adoption problem is now
benchmarked on real schemas: JSONSchemaBench~\cite{jsonschemabench}
measures constrained-decoding approaches across efficiency, coverage
and output quality, and the serving engines expose grammar backends
--- XGrammar~\cite{xgrammar}, Outlines~\cite{outlines} and the
guidance stack~\cite{guidance} --- that enforce a schema at decode
time. The SERVING-ABSENT refusal of the present study (the border
accepts the \code{response\_format} field and does not apply it to
the decode) was probed on pieces at the twenty-seventh wave and
re-probed as an endpoint-selection gate at the twenty-eighth: an
endpoint that accepts a schema without applying it is refused by
name, which is why the structured-diff campaign (D264/OPT-16) runs
its bounded repair loop client-side --- the closed schema holding the
edit diff at a 79.5-native-token median, the free-text writing
variance remaining the named residual. The benchmark's lesson cuts
both ways: constraint fidelity is a serving property to be measured,
never assumed.

\paragraph{Learned vectorisation and decompilation.}
Two adjacent lines sharpen the positioning. AutoVecCoder
~\cite{autoveccoder} teaches LLMs to emit \emph{explicitly vectorised}
source code (an automated vectorisation pipeline with a reinforcement
stage) --- their vectorisation is a source-level transformation, while
the SIMD corridors here are hand-written notation under seal, measured
against the tuned library (the 0.7271 cycles-per-byte interleaved
corridor) and scheduled against the compiler on the frozen VAES
notation (the notation-versus-C schedule gap published as a distinct
result at the twenty-eighth wave). On the decompilation side,
LLM4Decompile~\cite{llm4decompile} and SLaDe~\cite{slade} erode the
anti-reverse-engineering premium that the IP plan of the threat model
leans on --- the pack assumes it: the sealed notation's value is
governance and provenance, not secrecy, and the decompilation line is
cited to keep that assumption honest.

\paragraph{RL for code, and what the reward measures.}
SuperCoder~\cite{supercoder} optimises \emph{execution speed} by
reinforcement on runtime rewards --- the strongest published RL result
on assembly optimisation, and the natural contrast for the adoption
corpus's training plan: the reward there is the conformity judge's own
verdict (syntax, expansion, round-trip, oracle, mutation --- cumulative
and strict, never a similarity, never a runtime), because the property
being trained is the machine-writing stability that the free-text
refusals (HORS-FORMAT) name, not the speed of the artifact.
